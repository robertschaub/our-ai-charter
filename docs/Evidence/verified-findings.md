# Public AI Initiation — Verified Findings

Last full verification: 2026-06-23. Geneva-2027 entry updated 2026-07-17; the Apertus, motions, Geneva-2027 and Current AI anchors re-verified 2026-07-28.

## Bottom line

The strongest lever is **not a parliamentary instrument** but **docking onto existing, publicly funded, reputable structures** and bundling reach into a governance charter/alliance. Verified reason: a non-parliamentarian can trigger a Motion / Postulat / Parliamentary Initiative **only indirectly** via a council member; the only *direct* federal instrument is the petition, which legally compels nothing beyond acknowledgement. This supports the thesis: **alliance/mandate first, lawmaking later.**

## Verified facts

### Apertus / Swiss AI stack — the technical anchor already exists
- Fully open Swiss LLM by EPFL, ETH Zurich and CSCS; Apache 2.0; open weights, training data and methods; publicly funded (ETH-Board investment + >10m GPU hours on CSCS Alps; a further CHF 20m — EPFL attributes it to the **ETH Domain**, secondary reporting frames it as backing the initiative through 2028 — supports the next phase); released 2 Sept 2025; **Apertus 1.5 released 24 Jul 2026** (multimodal input, 262,144-token context, optional reasoning; re-verified 2026-07-28 — nothing newer than 1.5 shipped). [[1]](#source-1)[[2]](#source-2)[[3]](#source-3)
- Embodies the trust criteria (provenance, transparent assumptions, reproducible methods) — artefacts are independently inspectable on Hugging Face / GitHub, not merely described. [[1]](#source-1)[[4]](#source-4)
- **Governance — open, not a deficiency:** Publicly, Apertus's governance appears lean (Steering Committee + dev team); an independent multistakeholder board or public *governance* charter is **not publicly evident** (internals unverified — see open questions; note the **"Apertus Charter"** released with 1.5 on 2026-07-24 — formerly "Swiss AI Charter" — is a model-values/alignment document, a different layer, and does not close this gap). *Whether and how* such a layer should be added is an open question this contribution can help clarify — to be raised as a question to SNAI/Apertus, not as criticism. [[1]](#source-1)[[3]](#source-3)
- Institutional home is real: the **Swiss AI Initiative** (the programme, Dec 2023) and the **Swiss National AI Institute (SNAI)** — a partnership of the ETH AI Center and the EPFL AI Center; 800+ researchers, 10+ institutions. (Two distinct things: the Initiative is the Apertus programme; SNAI is the institute.) [[3]](#source-3)[[5]](#source-5)

### Swiss parliamentary instruments — access is the binding constraint
- Motion / Postulat / Parliamentary Initiative: **only** a council member, parliamentary group or committee may submit. No direct citizen route at federal level. [[6]](#source-6)
- Postulat = the right lightweight instrument for a governance/clarification ask: commissions the Federal Council to *examine and report*; needs only one chamber. (A Motion is binding and needs both chambers.) [[6]](#source-6)
- Petition = the only instrument a private person can trigger **directly** (any person, no minimum signatures, any topic) — but compels only acknowledgement, so weak as a governance lever. [[7]](#source-7)[[8]](#source-8)

### International anchors — dock, don't found
- **Current AI:** global multistakeholder public-interest-AI partnership founded at the Paris AI Action Summit (Feb 2025; ~USD 400m initial commitment, goal ~USD 2.5bn over five years; governments + philanthropies + industry — self-reported, currentai.org). **Switzerland is already a Country Partner** (Paris Charter signatory). *Re-verified 2026-07-28:* a TechCrunch profile (2026-07-19) confirms the $400m as the original Feb-2025 pledge (no new round) and reports the first grant cohort — $3.2m across four organisations — plus the AlphaChat launch at AI for Good Geneva (corroborated). [[9]](#source-9)
- **CERN for AI** (Centre for Future Generations blueprint): names Switzerland a "natural founding member" (with UK, Canada) of an **EU-led** structure — a participant, not its initiator; swissinfo reports Switzerland "risks exclusion." Supplies concrete anti-capture mechanisms: political representatives limited to high-level strategy, two oversight boards outside the executive, audit parts made public. [[10]](#source-10)[[15]](#source-15)

### Live political window
- **Motion 24.3209 Charles Juillard (Die Mitte / JU), "Für eine souveräne digitale Infrastruktur in der Schweiz im Zeitalter der künstlichen Intelligenz"**: tabled 2024 (Federal Council recommended rejection, 22 May 2024); **approved by the Council of States 31:11 (March 2026)**; **now in the National Council committee** (status June 2026). An open political thread to attach a governance/trust layer to — Juillard's motion targets *infrastructure/cloud*; the complement here is the *governance/trust* layer. Corroborated across multiple sources (Curia Vista 24.3209, Walder Wyss/datenrecht.ch, Parldigi, netzwoche, inside-it). [[11]](#source-11)[[16]](#source-16)[[17]](#source-17) **Re-verified 23 Jun 2026 (secondary trackers; Curia Vista not directly reachable): status unchanged. Re-verified 2026-07-28 (Curia Vista read directly): Stand der Beratungen «In Kommission des Nationalrats» — unchanged; SR adoption date per the official chronology: 19.03.2026.**
- **Motion 26.3221 Heidi Z'graggen (Die Mitte / UR), "Impulsprogramm zur Stärkung der digitalen Souveränität der Schweiz"** (Curia Vista AffairId 20263221): a **time-limited, targeted seed-funding** impulse programme for pilot projects across **four fields — digital infrastructure, open-source technologies, cybersecurity, and artificial intelligence** — activating existing state/industry/science competencies, framed explicitly as *not* an expansion of the state (no permanent subsidies). Federal Council recommended rejection (sovereignty "already addressed" via *Digitale Schweiz 2026* / *Digitale Bundesverwaltung*; budget limits); **adopted by the Council of States 30:7 (1 abstention), 9 June 2026** (Curia Vista chronology: 09.06.2026; media reports ran 10 June) — now before the National Council (a motion binds the Federal Council only once *both* chambers adopt it). The AI / open-source field is a natural docking point for a public-interest governance/evaluation layer — engagement note: [Digital-sovereignty motions (24.3209 + 26.3221)](../Strategy/digital-sovereignty-motions.md). Verified 23 Jun 2026 via the motioner's page + inside-it / Netzmedien / VSHN (Curia Vista not directly reachable, HTTP 403). **Re-verified 2026-07-28 (Curia Vista read directly): Stand «Zugewiesen an die behandelnde Kommission» (second-chamber pre-examination). The Federal Council's 27.05.2026 response also stated that the EMBAG start-up financing (Anschubfinanzierung) for digital projects is being dropped under the Entlastungspaket 2027 — a headwind for the funding side of the impulse.** [[21]](#source-21)[[22]](#source-22)[[23]](#source-23)[[24]](#source-24)
- **Geneva AI Summit 2027 — next global AI summit, Switzerland as host:** Successor in the AI Action/Impact Summit series (Bletchley 2023 → Seoul 2024 → Paris 2025 → New Delhi 2026 → **Geneva, 21–22 June 2027** → UAE 2028); announced by Federal President Guy Parmelin at the New Delhi summit (19 Feb 2026). Organised jointly by **UVEK** (parent department of BAKOM) and **EDA**; EDA project lead **Amb. Markus Reubi**, with **Amb. Thomas Schneider (BAKOM/OFCOM)** representing Switzerland in international AI bodies. Switzerland frames itself as neutral convenor and a sectoral "middle way" between the EU AI Act and lightly-regulated approaches. Preparation underway: invite-only roundtable 1 Apr 2026 and an open written call whose findings went to the *Plateforme Tripartite* on 13 Apr 2026; the next written-input window is not yet publicly dated (update 2026-07-17: the official *Road to Geneva* independent-pre-events programme opens registration ~Aug 2026 — see [channel demand](geneva-2027-channel-demand.md); update 2026-07-28: OFCOM re-confirmed 21–22 Jun 2027 at Palexpo and pinned the *Road to Geneva* launch to **August 2026** via the summit website — registration is a form-plus-approval step with **no stated deadline**, a "Call for Issues" follows in autumn 2026 and side-event proposals in early 2027; the first **UN Global Dialogue** session ran 6–7 Jul 2026 in Geneva, with the second scheduled for May 2027 in New York). (The "consultation draft due end-2026" sometimes cited is the *separate* Council of Europe AI Convention domestic implementing-law track — FDJP with DETEC/FDFA — not the summit.) NB: distinct from the annual ITU "AI for Good" Geneva summit. [[12]](#source-12)[[18]](#source-18)[[19]](#source-19)[[20]](#source-20)
