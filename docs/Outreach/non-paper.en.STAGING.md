# Swiss Contribution to Internationally Anchored Public AI Infrastructure

*Informal discussion paper (non-paper) - 2 pages*

_German STAGING version: [non-paper.de.STAGING.md](non-paper.de.STAGING.md). Earlier German source: [non-paper.de.md](non-paper.de.md)._

## 1. Problem statement

AI base infrastructure - models, compute, data, and the interfaces through which people use them - is increasingly concentrated among a small number of geopolitical and commercial actors. For free, rule-of-law societies this creates two linked risks.

First, public institutions, researchers, media, civil society, and businesses become dependent on opaque systems that can be priced, restricted, redirected, or withdrawn by actors they do not govern. Second, society loses the ability to judge AI outputs independently, because data provenance, assumptions, methods, dependencies, and control powers are often not open to public inspection.

This is also an economic problem. Capability, skills, bargaining power, and value creation concentrate where the infrastructure is controlled. A public-AI layer that keeps capability and economic benefit broadly distributed is not only a complement to governance; it is part of what makes co-stewardship durable and gives it a democratic mandate.

## 2. Switzerland's opening

Switzerland has unusually strong assets for a public-AI governance and evidence contribution:

- **Technological and scientific:** With *Apertus*, a fully open, publicly funded Swiss language model already exists (EPFL, ETH Zurich, CSCS; Apache 2.0). Weights, training data, code, and methods are transparently accessible.
- **Institutional:** The Swiss AI Initiative / Swiss National AI Institute (SNAI), a partnership of the ETH and EPFL AI Centers, connects more than 800 researchers across more than 10 institutions, alongside the Swiss National Supercomputing Centre (CSCS).
- **Diplomatic:** As host of the Geneva AI Summit 2027, Switzerland can act as a neutral convenor and bridge-builder.
- **Internationally anchored:** Switzerland is already a Country Partner of *Current AI*, the Paris-based partnership for public-interest AI.
- **Political:** In 2026 the Council of States adopted two relevant motions: 24.3209 on sovereign digital infrastructure and 26.3221 on an impetus programme for digital sovereignty, including open source and AI. Both are now before the National Council. They strengthen the case for building; the governance and evidence layer is still open.

The strongest Swiss contribution is therefore not another isolated national model. It is a credible host-and-node role for internationally co-stewarded public AI infrastructure: open, inspectable, multilingual, scientifically grounded, rights-respecting, and protected from political or commercial capture.

## 3. The missing layer: governance plus evidence

Trust does not come from a "Made in Switzerland" label alone. It comes from traceable data provenance, transparent assumptions, reproducible methods, clear accountabilities, and reviewable control powers.

For agentic and frequently updated systems, assurance must also be lifecycle-based. A point-in-time review is not enough. Good assurance runs before deployment, through runtime monitoring and constraint, and after release through incident reporting, correction, surveillance, and reassessment after material change. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework applies risk management across design, development, use, and evaluation; for high-risk systems, the EU AI Act adds post-market monitoring and serious-incident reporting duties.

The Charter contribution is an open specification for this governance and evidence layer. In Phase 1 it is a public draft and contribution to existing efforts, not a new institution, certification body, audit authority, or mark.

## 4. Governance principles

- Traceable provenance of data, models, methods, dependencies, and financial resources.
- Transparent assumptions and reproducible evaluation methods.
- Separation between strategic oversight and day-to-day operations; political representation belongs at the strategy level, not in operational execution.
- Independent oversight outside a single participant, supplemented by public audit summaries where disclosure is lawful.
- Assurance beyond self-reporting: attestation, audit or sampling rights, penalties for false attestation, and a named adjudicator outside any single participant.
- Lifecycle monitoring: material-change logs, runtime evidence, incident handling, correction records, withdrawal triggers, and continuity or exit plans where public reliance is material.
- Transparent, majority public or non-profit financing. Commercial partners are disclosed but exercise no controlling function.
- International co-stewardship from the beginning, with meaningful routes for inspection, challenge, and contribution.

## 5. Concrete contributions toward Geneva 2027

The immediate goal is a bounded package that can dock into existing public-AI and Geneva 2027 work:

1. **Governance blueprint** - roles, anti-capture rules, conflict-of-interest controls, oversight, adjudication, public evidence, and rules for restriction, recall, shutdown, or external pressure.
2. **Evaluation and accountability practice** - an open grounding-faithfulness and contestability method for deployed factual systems: do cited claims match the sources, does the system abstain rather than bluff, can errors be corrected, and can control powers be reviewed?
3. **Pilot path on existing infrastructure** - a model-plural public layer through which reviewable public-interest models, including Swiss open infrastructure where partners agree, can be reached and used with accountabilities attached.
4. **Coalition and roundtable path** - a compact group of Swiss and international partners to test whether this package should become a Geneva 2027 contribution.

## 6. What this initiative is not

- Not a new language model competing with SNAI/Apertus or other public models.
- Not a Swiss go-it-alone solution.
- Not an anti-industry campaign.
- Not a new certification body, audit authority, or trust mark.
- Not a claim that any system is already Charter-aligned.

## 7. Specific asks

**For Public AI / Metagov peers:** Is this governance and evidence layer a useful contribution *into* the Public AI movement, or does it duplicate work already underway? If useful, where should it dock: the Public AI Inference Utility, one of Current AI's Fund / Build / Bridge pathways, a neutral public report index, or another existing effort?

**For AI Verify / Project Moonshot and technical evaluation partners:** Could the grounding-faithfulness and contestability method become a portable recipe or benchmark task, wrapping existing detectors and human adjudication rather than replacing them? The immediate need is a concrete technical read: minimum inputs, query-set design, rating workflow, anti-gaming controls, and what a credible first pilot would require.

**For philanthropic public-AI funders:** This is not a funding ask at this stage. The useful question is: what evidence, partners, governance safeguards, and first deliverable would need to exist before work like this becomes fundable or worth introducing to partners?

**For all three:** The near-term request is one focused exchange, one candid critique, and, if warranted, one introduction to the right technical or governance counterpart.
