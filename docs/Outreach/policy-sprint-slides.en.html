Policy-sprint proposal · Scoping

An international network of open AI models with a common charter

Common, verifiable charter · independent evidence layer · Switzerland as hub, host and bridge-builder.

Short presentation & handout. Depth: 1-pager · Non-Paper.

1 · The goal

An international network of open AI models whose participants verifiably commit to a common charter. In doing so, it protects and strengthens free and fair societies in the digital age and fosters their resilience and self-determination. As the network's key international hub, host and bridge-builder, Switzerland in turn gains in standing and economic strength; a coordinating role can emerge from that. Switzerland can catalyse this build-up with a national impulse — by which route (a policy sprint, an impulse programme in the sense of Motion 26.3221, or another) is deliberately left open.

2 · The sprint question

How can Switzerland catalyse the build-up of an international network of open AI models whose participants verifiably commit to a common charter — with what sponsorship, what governance and funding criteria, and what first realistic steps? What role does Switzerland play as hub, host and bridge-builder, and can a coordinating role emerge from that?

Scoping ladder: goal → route & sponsorship (charter, governance, selection) → concrete building block.

3 · Why now

  • Political window: Council of States 2026 — Motion 26.3221 (impulse programme, 9 Jun) and 24.3209 (sovereign infrastructure, 19 Mar); both now before the National Council. They strengthen the build — purpose, governance, networking remain open.
  • Building blocks in place: Apertus (fully open) · Switzerland is a Current AI Country Partner · Swiss AI Action Plan (23 actions).
  • Timing: Geneva AI Summit 2027 — and in July 2026 the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance (6–7 Jul) and ITU AI for Good (7–10 Jul) met in Geneva.
  • Time pressure: something tangible by the summit (June 2027) — a process, blueprint and charter draft others can carry.

5 · Building blocks

  • Common charter, verifiably binding: purpose limitation, provenance, accountabilities, contestability, exit capability.
  • Open models in a network — interoperability, provider switching; complements Apertus, not a competing model.
  • Rights-cleared data and provenance commons — open sub-corpora where robust; protected content through research, audit or licensed access; shared clearance instead of one-off negotiations.
  • Independent governance and sponsorship — roles, oversight outside any single participant, transparent financing.
  • Swiss hub role — host and bridge-builder; a coordinating role can emerge.

Short: models, access and declarations exist — the independent, charter-bound evidence is missing. That is where the contribution sits.

7 · Possible sprint output

Programme parameters · governance/charter outline · selection/funding criteria · a postulate/interpellation or a preparatory mandate for a pre-sprint.

Realism: implementation/piloting by Geneva 2027 is a later, coalition-carried step. Contribution until then = process, blueprint, charter draft.

8 · Who is behind this

  • Our AI Charter — a public governance-and-evidence framework for AI systems (obligations, verifiability, contestability).
  • FactHarbor — an open, non-profit platform idea for structured evidence models.
  • FactHarbor association (Verein) — the non-profit Swiss carrier, with independence rules.

Stance: process and coalition, not authority — no certification/audit body, no ready coalition, no "already charter-aligned" claim.
