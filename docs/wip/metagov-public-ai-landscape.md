# Metagov & Public AI — landscape for the assurance & certification building block

*What Metagov is, who runs its AI-governance work, and where the Charter's [AI Assurance & Certification](../About.md) building block sits among the ecosystem's adjacent projects — for engaging the Public AI ecosystem (including its open [Fellow (Switzerland)](https://publicai.network/jobs/fellow-switzerland) role) and for Charter positioning.*

## TL;DR

- **Metagov (the Metagovernance Project) is the incubator behind the Public AI Network** — the coalition the open Fellow (Switzerland) role sits within. Public AI is "one of the major Metagov research areas," and that role's contact, **Joshua Tan**, is a Metagov co-founder and a Public AI Research Director. Understanding Metagov *is* understanding the coalition behind that role.
- **The Charter's opening, in one line:** across the whole ecosystem — free inference, sovereign models, benchmarks, deliberation — there is **no assurance/certification layer for *trustworthy public AI, accountable to people*.** "Accountable" is delivered through *ownership and governance*, not through *independent evidence*. That is exactly the [AI Assurance & Certification building block](../About.md).
- **The neighbours to differentiate from** are evaluation (Weval, Community-Aligned Benchmarks), technical safety (Atlas Computing), and one narrow certification precedent (Calm Tech Certified) — **allies, not competitors**. Name them correctly and the Charter reads as the missing layer, not a duplicate.
- **Closest internal ally: Atlas Computing / Evan Miyazono** — who is *also* Metagov's Research Director for AI governance. **Best "it can be done here" precedent: Calm Tech Certified** — a working certification standard already running inside Metagov.

## 2. The assurance & certification adjacency map

**My read:** these are the ecosystem projects nearest the Charter's building block. None is an assurance/certification regime for *trustworthy public AI* — so each is an ally to differentiate from cleanly, not a competitor.

| Project (who) | What it is | How the Charter differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Weval** — [weval.org](https://weval.org/); Collective Intelligence Project / Divya Siddarth; featured in Metagov's [Nov 2025 AI issue](https://metagov.substack.com/p/metagov-news-special-ai-issue-nov) | "Wikipedia for benchmarks" — an open, collaborative library of model *evaluations* | Crowd evaluation ≠ certification: the Charter is a *trust judgement against a standard, accountable to people* |
| **Community-Aligned AI Benchmarks** — [Aspen Digital](https://www.aspendigital.org/project/ai-benchmarks/); Public AI's *current* focus | Reimagine ML benchmarks to "encode public values" | Benchmarks measure *capability*; certification attests *trustworthiness* |
| **Atlas Computing** — [atlascomputing.org](https://atlascomputing.org/); Evan Miyazono (also Metagov's AI-gov RD) | "Provable safety" + scaling *human review* of AI via formal specs ("Guaranteed-Safe AI") | **Closest ally.** Atlas = formal/technical guarantees; Charter = human-accountable certification. Complementary. Read Miyazono's ["Govern AI with Rules, Not Values"](https://blog.atlascomputing.org/p/govern-ai-with-rules-not-values) |
| **"Meltdown" / "regulative AI"** — Zargham & Ben-Meir ([Nov 2025 AI issue](https://metagov.substack.com/p/metagov-news-special-ai-issue-nov)) | Systems to "interpret, coordinate, verify, and validate" generative output | A thesis, not a certification mechanism — a conceptual cousin to name |
| **Calm Tech Certified™** — Calm Tech Institute / Amber Case (a Metagov project; [2024 review](https://metagov.substack.com/p/metagovs-2024-year-in-review)) | A *working certification standard* — certified its first six companies in 2024 | Narrow domain (attention / calm tech), but the **operating-model precedent**: Metagov already hosts a certification scheme |
| **Public-AI procurement standards** — Beth Noveck ([publicai.substack](https://publicai.substack.com/p/who-will-shape-ai-in-the-public-interest), Mar 2026) | Six minimum public-procurement demands: portability · interoperability · transparency · **audit & evaluation rights** · pricing clarity · governance hooks | Procurement leverage on the *buyer* side; the Charter certifies the *system* — the "audit & evaluation rights" demand is the natural hook between the two |

## 3. The gap = the Charter's opening (precisely stated)

**Fact:** Public AI's four stated pillars are *publicly accessible · publicly accountable · a permanent public good · built now* ([publicai.network](https://publicai.network/)). "Accountable" is delivered through **public ownership and governance** — not through independent evidence or certification.

**My read (defensible, non-absolute):** the ecosystem has built the **access** layer (the [Public AI Inference Utility](https://publicai.co/) serving Apertus + SEA-LION) and is building the **evaluation** layer (Weval, Community-Aligned Benchmarks). The **assurance** layer — independent, human-accountable attestation that a public-AI system is *trustworthy*, at stated scope / depth / freshness — is the piece no one owns. The nearest instruments are either **narrow** (Calm Tech = attention), **technical** (Atlas = formal/provable safety), or **evaluation, not certification** (Weval, benchmarks). That is the [AI Assurance & Certification building block](../About.md), and it matches the summit's documented "beyond declarations" demand ([channel demand](../Evidence/geneva-2027-channel-demand.md)).

**The gap holds across the field's competing definitions of "public AI."** The two leading policy framings define the term differently — Open Future's [white paper](https://openfuture.eu/publication/white-paper-on-public-ai/) (Tarkowski et al., May 2025) as *public-interest* systems across a **compute / data / model** stack (on a "gradient of publicness"); Vanderbilt's [*Global Rise of Public AI*](https://law.vanderbilt.edu/vpa-releases-new-paper-on-the-global-rise-of-public-artificial-intelligence/) (Sitaraman & Parek, May 2025) as *government-led* infrastructure, sorted into four models — **outsourced provision · networked collaboration · state-corporate fusion · public options** — across nine states. Both differ again from this repo's *organisational* reading (coalition / utility / cooperative; [Apertus note](apertus-fit-and-engagement-plan.md) §1). **Yet neither white paper specifies any audit, certification, or independent-oversight mechanism** — each asserts "accountability" at the level of principle. That convergence is external corroboration that the assurance layer is a real, unfilled gap, not a niche concern.

This is the same *openness ≠ independent verification* thesis the [Apertus note](apertus-fit-and-engagement-plan.md) reaches from the model side — here reached from the ecosystem-map side.

## 4. Positioning the Charter with Public AI / Metagov

**My read** — three grounded moves for positioning the Charter in conversation with the coalition:

- **Lead with the assurance gap** (§3) as the differentiated contribution: it operationalises the "publicly accountable" pillar the coalition asserts but does not yet build.
- **Name the neighbours correctly** (Weval, Atlas, Community-Aligned Benchmarks) — signals knowledge of the landscape, not the slogans, and positions the Charter as *complementary*.
- **Cite the two internal proof-points**: Miyazono / Atlas (Metagov already funds provable-safety / human-review) and Calm Tech Certified (Metagov already runs a certification standard) — both say *this direction is welcome here*.
