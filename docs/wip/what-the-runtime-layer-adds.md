# What the runtime layer adds — and which discipline it belongs to

A recurring classification objection in public discussion of the Charter's runtime model (August 2026) runs: the runtime gate is *"classical control engineering, not governance"* — policies and authority boundaries are defined upstream in the engineering and certification lifecycle, the runtime component simply implements and enforces them, and the vocabulary around it ("runtime governance", "authority", "admissibility") is terminology in search of a mechanism. Its sharpest form is a question: **what problem is the runtime layer intended to solve that is not already addressed by established control engineering and certified configuration management?** ([Alexander Strüver's article of 1 August 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/real-ai-safety-problem-runtime-certification-alexander-str%C3%BCver-iflec), [his reply of 2 August](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:ugcPost:7489343727761793024/?dashCommentUrn=urn%3Ali%3Afsd_comment%3A%287489635253192577024%2Curn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7489343727761793024%29&dashReplyUrn=urn%3Ali%3Afsd_comment%3A%287489637898997161984%2Curn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7489343727761793024%29), and [his article of 7 August](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-governance-confusing-engineering-alexander-str%C3%BCver-yxwoc/) state this position most explicitly; the [VBE crosswalk](justice-tree-vbe-charter-crosswalk.md) and the *Wrong Layer* debate sit in the same discussion family.)

This note answers the question once, so replies can cite one argument instead of re-deriving it.

## What the Charter accepts in the classification

The component is engineered enforcement, and the Charter's own texts never claim otherwise. The gate decides *under* current policy and authority, not what policy should exist ([When Should Runtime AI Governance Interrupt?](../Published/when-should-runtime-ai-governance-interrupt.md)); "a deterministic authorization gate can prove that declared rules were applied; it cannot by itself establish that those rules are lawful, fair, or legitimate" ([Charter Commitments](../Assurance/Framework/charter-commitments.md), consequential-action baseline); a compliant gate does not establish legitimacy; and perfect enforcement establishes deterministic execution of predefined constraints — bounded unattended operation, not autonomy. None of that is contested.

What is contested is the problem class. "Control engineering plus configuration management" names two disciplines, and the gate's job belongs to neither.

## The discipline map

| Layer | Question it answers | State it consults | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Governance** (institutional) | What may be required; who is accountable; who remedies | Law, policy, society | The *Govern* function of the [NIST AI RMF](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/); the [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng); the [five separated roles](../Published/when-vs-who-ai-governance.md) |
| **Certification & configuration management** | Is the running system the approved system and policy version? | Design baselines, versions | Type approval and conformity assessment (precedents below) |
| **Runtime assurance / control engineering** | Is this proposed *physical* transition safe and stable, and is a fallback needed? | Plant state, physical envelope | The Simplex/runtime-assurance pattern — assured monitors plus fallback ([Fenn, Nicholson, Pai & Wilkinson 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220016762/downloads/fnpw-SSS2023-final.pdf)) |
| **Runtime authorization & policy enforcement** — the gate | May *this actor* take *this exact action, now*? | **Current** mandate, delegation and revocation state, cumulative counters, nonces, plus the frozen proposal | Decision/enforcement separation in [XACML 3.0](https://docs.oasis-open.org/xacml/3.0/xacml-3.0-core-spec-os-en.html) (2013); [NIST SP 800-207](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/207/final) tenets 3, 4 and 6; [RFC 9396](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9396.html); [NCCoE agent identity & authorization](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/projects/software-and-ai-agent-identity-and-authorization) — open questions |
| **Accountability infrastructure** | What happened; who answers; can it be contested and remedied? | Sealed action records, custody, review routes | EU AI Act Arts. 14, 72, 73; [split custody](split-custody-per-action-records.md); the five roles again |

Control engineering regulates a plant against physics. Authorization regulates an actor against a mandate. Certification proves the evaluator is the approved one. The objection names rows two and three; the gate sits in row four; the Charter's distinctive work is row five. One rule is worth importing from the runtime-assurance tradition rather than disputing: there, the wrapper must be assured to a *higher* level than the complex function it constrains — for the Charter, the authorization service should carry at least the assurance of the acting path it gates.

## Authority boundaries versus authority state

Authority *boundaries* are defined upstream: mandate schemas, policy rules and ceilings are versioned, approved and certified. On this the objection is right, and the Charter says the same thing.

Authority *state* is not upstream. Which mandates exist right now, whether a delegation has expired, whether Wednesday's revocation has landed, how much of a cumulative ceiling is already consumed, whether this ruling's nonce has been spent — these facts change through institutional acts at transaction cadence, with no engineering release and no re-certification event. Configuration management can prove that the approved system and policy version are running. It cannot prove that this actor currently holds authority for this exact payment, filing or disclosure, because that fact did not exist at certification time.

Security engineering treats use-time authority as its own discipline precisely because configuration-time knowledge cannot supply it. [NIST SP 800-207](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/207/final) defines zero trust around "accurate, least privilege per-request access decisions" and rejects treating a subject authenticated once as valid for every later request (tenets 3, 4 and 6). [RFC 9396](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9396.html) exists because type-level OAuth scopes cannot express "this transfer of this amount to this creditor". Certificate revocation infrastructure exists because certified artifacts become invalid without changing. And for AI agents specifically, the [NCCoE draft concept paper](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/accelerating-the-adoption-of-software-and-ai-agent-identity-and-authorization-concept-paper.pdf) (February 2026) still poses "What are the mechanisms for an agent to prove its authority to perform a specific action?", delegation for "on behalf of" scenarios, and non-repudiation binding back to human authorization as **open questions** — "already addressed" is not the standards body's own position.

So the component's discipline name is per-action **authorization and policy enforcement** — security engineering, not plant control — and the Charter's name for it is the **per-action authorization and evidence gate**. Certification governs the gate itself: its evaluator, its versioned rules, its fail-closed behaviour. What the gate consults is runtime institutional state that no certified configuration can contain.

**Where the public discussion converged.** Presented with this distinction, the objection's author agreed (2026-08-03) that "the runtime must verify the current authorization state before executing the action," classifying that function as "classical identity, access control, and authorization engineering" — row four of the map, under its security-engineering name — while keeping it distinct from physical admissibility and from autonomous capability; in the follow-up thread (2026-08-07) he described such mechanisms as "engineering mechanisms implementing governance requirements." That is this note's position stated from the other side: the component is engineered; the arrangement around it — who sets and may change the constraints, what evidence survives, who answers for what is permitted — is governance. The residual disagreement is attributional (which layer the word "governance" may name), not architectural. The full exchange is mirrored in the [runtime article's discussion record](../Published/when-should-runtime-ai-governance-interrupt.md#related-public-discussion).

## The capability boundary, stated as the Charter's own rule

The objection's strongest line — no amount of governance terminology, middleware or audit infrastructure can make a missing computational capability exist — is a rule the Charter already encodes rather than resists. Missing or ambiguous authority fails closed. A screening model's output can raise a concern but never authorize. "Not assessed" never waives a baseline duty, and evidence theatre is a named risk, not a compliance strategy. Stated once, generally: **where no available architecture can produce the evidence a consequence requires, that consequence is not available for that scope — the gap routes to engineering or research, never to more process.** The runtime layer does not manufacture capability; it prevents a missing basis from being papered over at the moment of effect.

## Precedent: requirements that become engineered, auditable controls

The objection's home domain — type-approved vehicles — already runs requirements-setting and engineered enforcement as one co-designed loop:

- **[UN Regulation No 155](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A42021X0387)** (cybersecurity and cybersecurity management systems; entry into force 22 January 2021) makes vehicle type approval conditional on an audited management system: the CSMS certificate of compliance is valid for at most three years, the approval authority may verify continued conformity at any time and withdraw the certificate, and the manufacturer's duties include managing supplier-related risks, implementing cybersecurity measures in the design, detecting and responding to attacks, and logging data to support detection.
- **[UN Regulation No 156](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A42021X0388)** (software updates and software update management systems; entry into force 22 January 2021) governs post-approval change: type-approval-relevant software carries a dedicated identifier (RXSWIN), every initial and updated software version must be uniquely identifiable including integrity validation data, and the RXSWIN state before and after an update must remain accessible — versioned change under governance without re-approving the vehicle from scratch.
- **[FDA, Predetermined Change Control Plans](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/04/2024-28361/marketing-submission-recommendations-for-a-predetermined-change-control-plan-for-artificial)** (final guidance, 4 December 2024) brings the same pattern to AI directly: a marketing submission for an AI-enabled device software function may include the planned modifications, the methodology to develop, validate and implement them, and an impact assessment — reviewed once, so that pre-specified modifications can be implemented "without necessitating additional marketing submissions".

These regimes show that requirements-setting stays institutional while becoming operative and auditable only through engineered controls, and that certification frameworks already anticipate versioned post-approval change. What none of them contains is rows four and five of the map: no per-action authority decision against current mandate state, and no per-action record, challenge route or remedy for the person affected. That is the layer the Charter adds.

## Terms, held precisely

- Where the Charter says **"runtime governance"**, it names the arrangement — an action-level enforcement point wired to upstream rulemaking and downstream custody, independent review and remedy — not the component. The component alone is enforcement; calling it governance without the institutions would deserve the objection.
- **No novelty claim** attaches to decision/enforcement separation itself: it has a dated antecedent in the 2013 XACML 3.0 architecture, as the [VBE crosswalk](justice-tree-vbe-charter-crosswalk.md) already records. The Charter's additions around those primitives are the mandate and authority-state semantics, the record sealed before effect, and the separated institutional roles.
- The published articles are verbatim mirrors of the live LinkedIn texts; whether to carry a one-sentence definition of "runtime governance" into the live articles is a separate maintainer decision, not made here.
